# D9. Component models (M3; resolves TSD §12)

Decision D9 of the OpenSolve Pipe decision record ([index](README.md)). Decisions override the specs; `docs/GOALS.md` overrides decisions. Code and tests cite this decision as `CLAUDE.md D9`.

---

- **Tees and wyes:** Crane TP-410 correlations in `fluids.fittings` (`K_branch/run_diverging/converging_Crane`) on the combined-flow velocity. They cover only a combined flow through a run port: a tee with the combined flow through its branch uses Darby 3-K "Tee, through-branch (as elbow)" per path and is flagged; a wye in that pattern has no cited correlation and is reported as not converged. The wye's lateral leans toward `outlet_1`. `lossModel: 'ideal'` gives EPANET-style junctions. *(GOALS §4.1: no uncited K.)*
- **Fluids:** CoolProp 8.0.0 (pinned): `Water` (IAPWS-95), `INCOMP::MEG`/`INCOMP::MPG` (Melinder 2010) within CoolProp's queried ranges. Glycols have no vapor pressure in CoolProp, so NPSH needs a user value (flagged otherwise).
- **Pump curves:** monotone piecewise cubic (`scipy.interpolate.PchipInterpolator`), straight end-slope extrapolation (flagged), affinity laws for speed. No viscosity correction (Later); the trace states it.
- **Valves:** default K from the Crane function for the valve type at the inlet pipe's diameter; Cv/Kv via `fluids` (ISA-75.01.01). No position-dependent K and no control-valve Cv curve in v1 (no cited characteristic); control valves default to ideal (zero loss when wide open). PRV/PSV setpoints are static pressures at the valve port. A valve whose K on its inlet pipe, or c = K/(2gA²), is not a finite double (a Cv near 10⁻¹⁷⁰), or whose c is finite but above half the largest double, 8.988465674311579·10³⁰⁷ (`coefficients.SLOPE_COEFFICIENT_MAX`; a Cv near 10⁻¹⁵⁰ on 0.1 m), is the topology issue `VALVE_RESISTANCE_OUT_OF_RANGE`: `ValveLaw` forms its slope 2c\|Q\| as (2c)·\|Q\|, and doubling a double is exact unless it overflows, so past the bound the slope is +∞ at every flow but zero and NaN at zero, whatever the network, the start or the iteration limit, and wherever the law is evaluated the solve could only fail; every c up to it is evaluated (one at the bound converged in 505 iterations from 1 ft/s with `maxIterations` 1000). The same bound, from the same derivation, refuses equipment (D18), an orifice plate's K (D20) and a fitting entry or a connection's fittings' sum (D22) (open item 2, 2026-11-03; a plate on its K at the upper bound of its discharge coefficient over every flow, which its law need not reach; D7 names the overflows these refusals leave: those that depend on the start or the flow, and a plate's K past the bound only at the C its law takes, which can be at every flow, D20). Each refusal holds wherever the element is, as it already did for a c that is not a finite double, so an element whose law the solve never evaluates is refused too: for example a valve isolated, locked closed or failed closed (a PRV, PSV or FCV), a PRV or PSV its status rules keep from opening (closed, or a PSV held active at zero flow), or a valve, equipment, a fitting or an orifice plate in a section that isolated, locked-closed or failed-closed valves close off. Such a project converged on `7fcbdb8` and is now a 422 (verified for each case named, with c, or a plate's K, from 1.2 to 1.7·10³⁰⁸), so the methods are 1.6.1 (GOALS §4.3; `test_an_isolated_valve_past_the_slope_bound_is_refused_though_never_evaluated`; D20's rule (e) refuses a plate the same way). Where the solve does evaluate such an element it fails there: the same valve or equipment item open in the main line diverged at the first iterate, as did a PRV or PSV held active at a flow, which its rules open since its fully open loss exceeds the head across it.
- **Status logic:** EPANET 2.2 rules translated to EGL, at most 30 outer iterations that change statuses or branch K (iterations that only hold or release a zero-flow tee leg are bounded separately), oscillation → `converged: false` naming the components. Deliberate departures from EPANET: an FCV closes on reverse flow and opens when the head across it is below its fully open loss; closed elements around a closed-off section are re-tested (difference constraints) so a pump and check valve that close together can reopen.
