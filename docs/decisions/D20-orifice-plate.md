### Model

`Orifice`, type `orifice`, ports `inlet` → `outlet`: a square-edged concentric plate, a restriction orifice (a minimum-flow bypass, balancing) or an orifice flow meter, one element for both (owner). A required `boreDiameter` d (m, (0, 10], no default); an optional `dischargeCoefficient`: absent, ISO 5167-2's Reader-Harris/Gallagher C at the solved flow, the cited default (labelled "default", D10); a number in (0, 1], an entered C from a calibration or datasheet, user data: ISO 5167-2 eq. (1)'s C at the plate's taps (at D and D/2 tappings for a plate without taps), held at every flow, not the flow coefficient α = C/√(1 − β⁴) (the schema, form and trace say so); optional `taps` (`corner`, `flange` or `d_and_d_2`): absent, a plate without taps, with no reading. No status (valves isolate it) and no Cv or Kv (owner): a restriction known only by a Cv, a Kv or a drop at a flow is D18's equipment, and both forms' hints say so. D is the inner diameter of the inlet pipe's end segment at the inlet port, as a valve's reference diameter (D9); β = d/D. Each value's only rule bounds it, so no new `x-` extension; `schemaVersion` stays 1 (an additive type, D14). *(GOALS §4.1: no source gives a default bore; ISO 5167-2 is the cited source of C.)*

### Law

h = EGL_in − EGL_out = K(C_L)·v\|v\|/(2g), v = Q/A on D (`services/solver/orifice.py`, `LinkKind.ORIFICE`): K = (S/(Cβ²) − 1)², S = √(1 − β⁴(1 − C²)), ISO 5167-2's pressure-loss relation over ρv²/2 (fluids `discharge_coefficient_to_K`), computed without its cancellation as β → 1 (K = u², u = (1 − β⁴)/(Cβ²(S + Cβ²)), 1 − β = (D − d)/D). **One loss per plate** (owner): C_L, the C that sets the loss, is the C at D and D/2 tappings whatever the plate's taps: ISO 5167-2:2003 eq. (4) (fluids `C_Reader_Harris_Gallagher`) at Re_eff = max(Re_D, Re_lo), or an entered C_e moved there, C_L = C_e·r with r = C_ISO(D and D/2)/C_ISO(taps) at the same Re_eff for corner or flange taps (C_e itself without taps or at D and D/2 taps), so a calibration equal to ISO's C gives ISO's answer. Below ISO's lowest Reynolds number, Re_lo = max(5000, 16000β²) (ISO's limit made continuous at β 0.56) or max(5000, 170000β²D) at flange taps, C is held at its value there and flagged (WANDA's practice); fluids is called with the mass flow whose Re_D is Re_eff, never at Q = 0 or below Re 5000. The slope is exact (eq. (4)'s analytic dC/dRe, dK/dC = −2K(1/C + β²/S)), zero flow takes the valve path (the Jacobian floor; the law stays exact), and `gga.py`, `status.py` and `system_curve.py` are unchanged. Named departures, stated in the methods summary and the trace at their largest over ISO 5167-2's range (D 50 mm–1 m, β 0.1–0.75, Re_D from Re_lo up): ISO's relation read with a meter's own C (a K up to 6.14 % higher at corner and 6.34 % at flange tappings, a flow up to 2.94 % and 3.03 % lower where the plate takes the whole head; up to β 0.6, 0.64 % and 0.84 % in K); Reader-Harris et al. 2019's C at L2′ = 0.125 (a K 0.063–1.24 % lower, a flow up to 0.63 % higher); the continuous Re_lo (stricter than ISO's by up to 17.6, only for 0.559 < β ≤ 0.56). *(GOALS §4.1: exact, and checkable by hand.)*

### Reading and vena contracta

A meter's `differentialPressure` = (1 − β⁴)/(C_M²β⁴)·ρv\|v\|/2, ISO 5167-2 eq. (1) with ε = 1 (liquids only), C_M ISO's C at the plate's own taps (held at their own Re_lo) or the entered C as entered; null without taps; it does not act on the network. p_vc = p_up + ρg(z_up − z_down) − (1 − β⁴)/(C_L²β⁴)·ρv²/2, p_up the static pressure of the port the flow enters and z_down the elevation of the port it leaves by (WANDA); below the vapor pressure (≤ 0 Pa absolute for a fluid without one) it raises `BELOW_VAPOR_PRESSURE` (invalid) on the component: a flashing jet chokes the flow, so the flow solved is too high. None at zero flow or in a floating section; incipient cavitation is not checked. *(GOALS §4.6.)*

### Limits, flagged and refused

`CORRELATION_OUT_OF_RANGE` (warning, one per inequality, `details.limit` naming it in SI): wherever eq. (4) is used (ISO's C, or a moved entered C), d < 12.5 mm, D outside 50–1000 mm, β outside 0.1–0.75 (ISO 5167-2's limits from fluids' documentation), and 0 < Re_D below Re_lo; for every plate, an outlet pipe of another bore than D (the relation assumes recovery in the inlet's bore) and reverse flow (the symmetric law on D); with an entered C, laminar flow, 0 < Re_D < 2000 in the inlet pipe, as a valve's Cv. The plate and its installation are assumed to meet ISO 5167-2 and are not checked. `ORIFICE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422, listed after `EQUIPMENT_RATING_OUT_OF_RANGE`, one per plate, quoting d, D, β and the bound broken), for a plate the law cannot represent at any flow: β ≥ 1; eq. (4), where used, able to exceed 1 (from about β 0.970 on ISO's C, lower with flange taps on a pipe below about 55 mm), the text saying what it is used for there (`Refusal.basis`): at D and D/2 tappings it sets the loss, and C above 1 would be a jet larger than the bore, a loss below Borda–Carnot's; at a meter's taps (the reading) or in an entered C's move it is eq. (4) extrapolated far beyond ISO's range; each with a remedy that works for the plate; an entered C moved from corner or flange taps able to exceed 1; a loss coefficient K/(2gA²) that is not a finite positive double. Each extreme over Re ∈ [Re_lo, ∞) is a closed-form bound (each Re-dependent term of eq. (4) at its safe end), so the refusal does not depend on the fluid. *(GOALS §4.6; D9's refusal where no cited correlation gives a physical value.)*

### Results and traceability

`SolvedState.orificeResults`, always present (`{}` without plates or when the solve did not converge): flow, headLoss, pressureLoss = ρg·headLoss (ISO 5167-2's permanent pressure loss), pressureDrop (static, as a valve's: ρgh + ρg(z_out − z_in) + ρ(v_out² − v_in²)/2, D7), differentialPressure, venaContractaPressure, beta, pipeDiameter, boreDiameter, reynoldsNumber, dischargeCoefficient (C_L), coefficientSource, coefficientReynoldsNumber, tapCoefficientRatio, lossCoefficient, taps and meterDischargeCoefficient. A closed-off plate has no result; one in a floating section has its flow, differences, reading, K and C, and no p_vc. Its trace gives the inputs with their sources (ISO's C and no taps labelled default; an entered C with its definition), eq. (4) term by term at D and D/2 tappings and at the plate's taps, the hold, the move, K, h by the law and as solved, the reading, p_vc absolute and gauge, every term of the static-drop identity, and, marked not used, the K a meter's own C would give and the C and K at L2′ = 0.125; its notes state the departures, ε = 1, the ISO and installation assumptions, the recovered outlet pressure and each flag's inequality. An "Orifices" methods section after Equipment (its entered-C items only with an entered C) and one assumption appear only when the project has a plate. *(GOALS §4.2.)*

### Scenarios and goal seek

D15's allow-list gains `boreDiameter` and `dischargeCoefficient` (null sets ISO's C, written "ISO 5167-2's (the default)"); the taps are installation, the same in every case, and not listed. Goal seek varies the bore (log) and the C (linear, the C at the plate's taps, as entered; a case on ISO's C starts in the middle of the bounds), and builds the network at each bound of such a variable (D16); four goals, `orifice_flow`, `orifice_pressure_loss`, `orifice_differential_pressure` (published with `needs: ['taps']`: 400 `GOAL_NOT_APPLICABLE` on a plate without taps) and `orifice_pressure_drop`, which are also Compare's rows. D stays fixed in a goal seek (a pipe's `spec` is not a variable).

### Design checks

None of D19's checks applies to the plate: NPSH margin and the operating region are a pump's, and pipe velocity covers the pipes on both sides of it, not its bore (`test_orifice.py::test_no_design_check_applies_to_a_plate`).

### Designers' web defaults (reversible, D14)

One menu entry, "Orifice plate (ISO 5167-2)", in the group "Orifice plates: restriction orifices, flow meters", id prefix `O`; every path that creates one asks for the bore first. The form: the bore in the diameter unit with a read-only β line (D from the inlet pipe as the form resolves it; amber at β ≥ 1), ISO's C or an entered C with its definition in the hint, and optional taps (locked in a scenario). An Orifices results view after Equipment, a Build strip of flow, permanent loss and reading, Compare rows (relative for the flow, the loss and the reading, not the static drop), goal-seek groups with default bounds [0.1 D, 0.75 D] for the bore and [v/2, min(2v, 1)] for the C (D from the inlet pipe as the case is now; with corner or flange taps every C of the box, moved, at most 0.99: the C at most 0.99/r_max, a bore box on an entered C bounded the same way, r_max decision 17 (c)'s bound from D, d and the taps, transcribed from the API in `utils/orifice-bound.ts` to pre-fill defaults only and checked against it by `test_web_orifice_r_max.py`), and an "Orifice plates" export sheet after Equipment with the bore and pipe bore each solve used, with About lines only in a file that has it.

### Versions

`METHOD_VERSION` 1.4.0: an orifice result names a version that has the plate (GOALS §4.3, as D18). Its changelog line says that projects without orifices are unchanged in every value; every link last solved with 1.3.0 shows the method-change notice after its next solve.

### Proof

17 reference cases, the first 15 by a tester who did not read `services/`, with an independent transcription of eq. (4) equal to fluids' within 10⁻¹³: 14 closed-form (C1–C12: a flange-tapped line, a meter read back by fluids' meter solver, an entered C, reverse flow, a plate outside ISO's geometry, a C held at low Re, the Reynolds dependence that a C held at another Re misses by 1.2–1.9 %, a balancing loop, a pump's minimum-flow bypass, flashing at the vena contracta, an outlet of another bore pinning the static-drop identity, and an entered C moved from corner taps, 2.52 % off if unmoved; then the review's C13, an entered C at flange taps below their Re_lo, flagged, and C14, reverse flow with the ports at different elevations, from the suite's own derivations), which the solver meets within 6×10⁻¹⁶ in flow and 6×10⁻¹⁵ m in EGL; 3 EPANET, each plate a GPV whose 201-point head-loss curve is the plate's own law (EPANET's flows 1.2×10⁻⁵ to 3.2×10⁻⁴ higher, relative); the calibration identity (ISO's C entered as the meter's C reproduces ISO's answer to 10⁻¹⁰), goal-seek inverses G33–G39 and scenario tests.
