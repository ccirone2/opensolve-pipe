## 3. Unit-aware entry (U1, U8)

`units.ts parseQuantity(text, category, displayUnit, {gauge, absolute})` reads a number, optionally followed by a unit, with or without a space: "100 ft", "100ft", "3 in", "4\"", "120 gpm", "36 m³/h", "68 °F", "50 psig", "300 kPa(g)", "3 barg".

- **Which units.** Every unit of the field's quantity in the registry: a length, a diameter and a head take m, mm, ft and in; a pressure Pa, kPa, MPa, bar and psi; the other categories their registry lists. A head typed as a pressure ("50 psi") is refused: converting needs the density. A dimensionless field takes no unit.
- **Spellings.** The registry's label and id, plus the spellings engineers type (`SPELLINGS`: feet, inch, ", ″, ”, ', ′, ’, gal/min, usgpm, lpm, cmh, m3/hr, fps, degF, pcf, cp, bhp, sq in, "° F", º, ft., U.S. gpm…), matched without regard to case except where case tells two things apart (`EXACT_SPELLINGS`): MPa (mega) from mPa·s (milli), m and mm from M and MM ("million" in US practice), K (kelvin) from k ("thousand"). A unit in the wrong case is refused with the right one: "“mpa”: mind the capitals, “MPa”."
- **Gauge and absolute (D4).** A gauge field takes "psig", "kPa(g)", "barg" or the bare unit; an absolute field "psia", "kPa(a)", "bara" or the bare unit. A value with the other mark is refused ("This is a gauge pressure (psig): an absolute value needs the atmospheric pressure subtracted first."); a pressure difference (a check valve's cracking pressure, a relief valve's set pressure, a rated pressure drop) takes no mark.
- **Shown.** The field stores the SI value and shows it in its own unit, the typed text replaced even when the value is the one already stored; a value typed in another unit is followed by "Converted from 12 m." until the next keystroke, another stored value or a change of the project's units, after which it is dropped for good (a redo does not bring it back). It is in the field's description, and said by a polite line the first conversion draws empty and writes two frames later (ui-guide.md, One strip of live lines), so nothing in a form is live before the user converts a value. The bounds are checked on the converted value.
- **Own unit only.** The NPS designation field (shown when the pipe library has no size list) takes inches alone: an NPS is a designation, not a length, so "100 mm" (DN 100) is refused, not converted to 3.94.
- **Unchanged.** A bare number is the field's unit, as before. Text that goes on as a number would ("1,5", "1.2.3", "1 000") keeps the not-a-number message; any other mark after the number is read as a unit and refused as one it does not take ("“˚F” is not a unit this field takes. Enter it in K, °C or °F.").
- **Phones.** A field whose display unit goes below zero (a temperature in °C or °F, though bounded above 0 K) opens the text keyboard, which has a minus sign; others open the decimal keypad, which has no letters, so there a unit is pasted rather than typed.

## 5. Round default lengths (U3)

`factory.ts defaultPipeLength(units)` and `shortPipeLength(units)`: 10 m and 1 m where lengths show in m or mm, 30 ft (9.144 m) and 3 ft (0.9144 m) where they show in ft or in (the owner's answer). ## 6. Paste a column or a table (U4)

`utils/paste-points.ts readPastedPoints` reads a paste into any cell of a curve's points table (the head curve, efficiency and NPSHr):

- **One value** (no tab, one line): the field takes it as typed text, so "300 gpm" pastes as before.
- **A column** fills the column pasted into, from that row down, adding rows as needed; the other column is kept, and points after the pasted ones keep their values in this column, as the note says ("Points 3 and 4 keep their earlier head.").
- **Two columns** are the curve from that row on, whichever cell was pasted into: the rows after the pasted ones are removed, as the note says ("The points after them (points 4 and 5) were removed."), so no point of the old curve is left mixed in unseen.
- **Separators.** Tabs (a spreadsheet's copy) when the text has any or starts with a quote, a cell holding a line break, a tab or a quote in quotes as Excel writes it; otherwise runs of ASCII spaces or semicolons (a data sheet copied from a PDF). A no-break or thin space is part of its cell, so "1 000" written with one is refused as a number. A comma in a value is refused: it can be a decimal mark, a thousands separator or a list, and guessing could store a value 1,000 times off (a comma in a heading, "Capacity, gpm", is read). Space-separated rows that could each be one number with its thousands grouped by a space (up to three digits, then three, with or without a decimal part: "1 000", "12 500.5") are refused unless a heading names two columns or a row's first cell starts with 0 (a group never does; a curve's shutoff row, "0 150", shows a table). A column grouped with points ("1.000" for one thousand, as some locales write) reads as decimals, as any number with a point does: the app reads a point as a decimal mark everywhere.
- **Empty cells.** Only the line breaks a spreadsheet adds after the last row are dropped. An empty line before or between rows, or an empty cell, is refused with its row ("row 2, head is empty"), never closed up: the values below it would move up a point onto the wrong flows.
- **Headings.** A first row is a heading when no cell of it starts as a number and it has a letter; a second such row is read with it (a data sheet's units on the line under its names, "Flow Head" then "(gpm) (ft)", or symbols and units, "Q [m³/h] H [m]"), as is a second row of units alone ("%", "(%)", "(-)"). A row such as "-" or "—" is data, refused as such. The heading is read whole, and every word must be accounted for: a column name this table knows (flow: Flow, Rate, Q, Capacity; head: Head, H, TDH; NPSHr: NPSH, NPSHr, NPSH3; efficiency: Efficiency, Eff, η), a point-number word ("#", "No.", "Pt.", "Point", or two of them, "Point No.") over a first column that is dropped, a word that names no column ("total", "developed", "required", "bowl", "US"…), or a unit (`units.ts namesUnit`); a name and a unit run together ("Q/gpm", "flow_gpm", "Flow-rate") or set off by a comma ("Capacity, gpm") are read as both. A word it cannot read ("MGD", "l/sec", "m^3/h", "mWC", "Imp gpm", "UK gpm", "Discharge", "n/a", "Shutoff") refuses the paste rather than being passed over, so a unit outside the registry is never read as the display unit. Its units, each in brackets ("Flow (m3/h) Head [m]") or each a word ("gpm ft"), never both, must be one per column, in order, each of its column's quantity: a heading naming another number ("Flow in gpm", where "in" is also inches) is refused, and so are empty brackets ("Efficiency ( )"), which name no unit. A dash standing alone between words is punctuation ("Pump - efficiency"); in brackets it is a unit. Its names, where it gives any, must name every column pasted, each over its own: cell by cell where the copy has tabs ("#\tFlow", "\tFlow" and "Discharge head\tFlow" are refused), line by line where it has spaces, each line naming every column in order or none and giving every column's unit or none ("Flow rate" is one name), so a first column's name wrapped onto a second line ("Required Flow (gpm)" over "NPSH (ft)") cannot pass for the second column's; "Head (ft) Flow (gpm)" and "Flow Efficiency" over a head curve are refused. A heading with no unit means the table's display units. A cell may carry its own unit (tab-separated), which must be the heading's where the heading names one.
- **Efficiency.** Shown in %; a heading unit "-", "1" or "fraction" in brackets ("Efficiency (-)", "η [-]"), or the word "fraction", reads the column as fractions, and a value over 1 is then refused with the reason. A column read in % with no unit anywhere whose values are all at most 1 % may be fractions (0.75 for 75 %), 100 times too small, and is refused with both headings to choose from.
- **Point numbers.** A first column of three is dropped only when its heading names point numbers ("#", "No.", "Point") and it counts 1, 2, 3…; without that heading three columns are refused, since a first column counting 1, 2, 3 may be flows.
- **All or nothing.** A paste of any length is read (130,000 rows were measured); one cell that does not read, one out of the schema's bounds, a heading it cannot read, ragged rows, more than two columns, or more rows than the table takes (50): nothing is pasted, and the line under the table says why, with the row and the column ("Nothing was pasted: row 2, flow, “abc”: Enter a number, e.g. 12.5 or 1.2e-3.").
- **Said.** After a paste the line under the table says what it read, in the units the values were in ("Pasted 4 points: flow in m³/h, head in m."; "flow in gpm and L/s" where cells differ), and which points were removed or kept their values, blank rows of a new curve not counted. The note shows only while the table holds the rows the paste made: an undo, another edit, or a point added or removed takes it away. It is said by a polite line the first paste draws empty, emptied at every paste and written two frames later (ui-guide.md, One strip of live lines), so the same words twice are said twice and nothing is live before a paste; the standing hint, that a column or a table can be pasted, is a separate line, never said. Every cell the paste writes drops its own unstored text, its error and its note, even where the value written is the one it held, so leaving a cell does not store that text over the pasted value. A new pump's head curve and its saved curve are one table, kept mounted when its third point saves the curve, so focus and the notes stay.
